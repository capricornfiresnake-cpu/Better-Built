Materials

What the pipework is made of.

Most housing stock runs to copper, PEX and — in older builds — galvanised steel on its way out. Each ages differently and each fails differently.

Copper

Long service life and easy to inspect. Pinhole leaks in older runs are usually water chemistry rather than bad workmanship.

PEX

Flexible, fast to install and fewer joints inside walls. The standard for repipes and most new residential work.

Brass and galvanised

Common in older homes. Galvanised narrows from the inside as it ages, which shows up as falling pressure long before it leaks.

Before you call

Four things worth having to hand.

None of this is required, but a call goes faster when you can answer these.

What the water is doing

Dripping, running, backing up, or gone entirely. Each points somewhere different.

Where the shut-off is

If you know where the main is, say so. If you do not, that is worth finding before anything urgent happens.

Roughly when it started

Sudden and gradual are different faults even when they look identical.

The age of the house

It narrows down what the pipework is likely to be, which narrows down the likely cause.

Common questions

What people usually ask.

General answers about this kind of work. Anything specific to your building is a phone call.

Does a damp patch always mean a burst pipe?
No. It often means a slow joint, a failed seal or condensation, and those are very different jobs. The point of a visit is to establish which before anything is opened up.
Is a repipe always the answer for old pipework?
No. Age alone does not condemn a system. Falling pressure across the whole house, repeated pinholes and visible corrosion together make a stronger case than any one of them alone.
How long does a water heater last?
Commonly eight to twelve years, and hard water shortens it. A unit that is rusting at the fittings or dropping temperature is usually nearer the end than the middle.
Can you find a leak without breaking anything?
Usually. Location work is done before demolition, not instead of a diagnosis, and the aim is to open the smallest area that solves the problem.

Still not sure what you are looking at?

Describe it on the phone. It usually takes a couple of minutes to work out whether it is a repair, a replacement, or nothing to worry about yet.
