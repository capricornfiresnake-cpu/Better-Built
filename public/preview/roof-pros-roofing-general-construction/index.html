Materials

What goes on roofs around here.

Inland North County runs mostly to tile and asphalt shingle, with standing seam metal increasingly common on newer builds. Each behaves differently in this climate and each fails differently.

Clay and concrete tile

Long service life and very heavy. Most tile failures are not the tile — they are the underlayment beneath it reaching the end of its life.

Architectural shingle

Dimensional asphalt, the most common re-roof in the region. Look for granules collecting in the gutter as the first sign of age.

Standing seam metal

Concealed fasteners and long panels. Expensive up front, and the longest-lived of the three by a wide margin.

Before you call

Four things worth having to hand.

None of this is required, but a call goes faster when you can answer these.

Roughly when it was built

Or when the roof was last replaced, if you know. Age narrows the likely cause more than anything else.

What you have actually seen

A stain on a ceiling, a dripping sound, a lifted tile, grit in the gutter. Where and when it appeared matters.

Whether it moves with the weather

A leak that only shows after wind-driven rain points somewhere different to one that shows in every shower.

Photographs, if you have them safely

From the ground or a window. Nothing is worth climbing up for.

Common questions

What people usually ask.

General answers about this kind of work. Anything specific to your building is a phone call.

Does a leak always mean a new roof?
No. Plenty of leaks come from flashing, a valley, a vent boot or a single displaced tile, and those are repairs. Age and how widespread the problem is are what usually decide between repair and replacement.
How long does a roof last in this climate?
It depends on the material and the underlayment. Asphalt shingle is typically measured in a couple of decades, tile far longer — though the underlayment beneath tile generally needs attention well before the tile itself does.
What does an estimate involve?
Generally someone looks at the roof or the site, works out what is actually going on, and puts a scope and a price in writing. What that includes varies between companies, so it is worth asking what you are getting.
Can roofing work happen in winter here?
Southern California weather allows work most of the year. Rain is the constraint rather than cold, which is why leaks tend to be discovered and repaired in the same short window.
What is the difference between a roofer and a general contractor?
A roofer works on the roof. A general contractor handles broader building work and coordinates trades across a project. Some companies do both, which matters if your job spans more than the roof.

Still not sure what you are looking at?

Describe it on the phone. It usually takes a couple of minutes to work out whether it is a repair, a replacement, or nothing to worry about yet.
